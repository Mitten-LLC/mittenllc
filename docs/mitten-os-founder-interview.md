# Teaching Mitten OS — Founder Interview

Answer these in your natural voice. Short stories and imperfect examples are
more useful than polished principles.

## How you frame the problem

1. When someone brings you an AI idea, what do you ask first—and what answer
   immediately makes you suspicious?
2. Tell me about a time the stated problem was not the real problem. How did you
   discover the difference?
3. What does “start with the customer experience and work backward” mean in
   your own words?
4. Which facts must you understand before recommending any technology?

## How you see the work

5. When you examine a workflow, what do you notice that other consultants tend
   to miss?
6. How do you distinguish a process problem, a product problem, a training
   problem, and a problem AI could genuinely help?
7. Where should human judgment remain, even when automation is technically
   possible?
8. What kinds of organizational constraints change an otherwise good idea?

## How you choose a first move

9. What makes an experiment small enough to be safe but meaningful enough to
   teach us something?
10. What evidence convinces you a pilot is useful? What metrics look impressive
    but tell you very little?
11. When would you tell a client not to use AI?
12. How do you decide whether to prototype, train, change the workflow, or stop?

## How you communicate

13. Share an example of a recommendation that sounds like you.
14. Share an example of generic consultant language you would never use.
15. When someone is excited but skipping important risks, how do you redirect
    them without killing momentum?
16. What do you want someone to feel and understand after a Mitten conversation?

## Boundaries and identity

17. Which parts of your method are universal, and which should remain specific
    to government, defense, testing, or training contexts?
18. What should Mitten OS never ask a public visitor to disclose?
19. What would make an AI-generated recommendation feel irresponsible to you?
20. If Mitten OS could remember only five rules from you, what would they be?
